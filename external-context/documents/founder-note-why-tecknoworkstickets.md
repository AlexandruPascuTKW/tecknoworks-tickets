*A note to the team from the founder & CEO. January 2026. Not a plan - a
description of what I want this to feel like.*

# The problem

Small and mid-sized organizers - a theatre company, a promoter running
three rooms, a conference team - are stuck between two bad options. The
big ticketing platforms take a large cut, own the relationship with the
audience and treat a 400-capacity room the same as a stadium. The cheap
tools can't do a seating plan, can't scan at the door and leave the box
office manager reconciling in a spreadsheet. We want to be the obvious
choice for the organizer who is serious but not huge.

# What it should feel like

## For the attendee

- Buying a ticket takes under 60 seconds on a phone. No account needed -
  an email address is enough, and your tickets arrive there.

- Every fee is visible from the first screen. If we'd be embarrassed to
  show a fee up front, we shouldn't charge it.

- No fake urgency. We will never show "only 3 left!" to push people.

- Up to 10 tickets in one go, so a group can sit together.

- Your ticket in your phone's wallet, not a PDF.

## For the organizer

- Sign up yourself on a Tuesday afternoon and be on sale by Tuesday
  evening. No sales call, no contract.

- Your audience is your audience: you can see who bought, and contact
  them about your events.

- Get paid fast and see exactly what we took and why.

- A door that works even when the venue's signal doesn't.

# Things I want us to get to

- Fair resale: if you can't go, sell your ticket to another fan through
  us, capped at face value plus 10%. Kills the touts' margin.

- Recommendations: "people who saw Hamlet also booked..." - AI-driven,
  eventually personalised.

- Membership presales: the Lantern's Friends get 48 hours before
  everyone else.

- Waiting lists for sold-out performances that release returned tickets
  automatically.

- Season tickets and bundles ("3 plays for €75").

Things I don't want: dynamic pricing that pushes prices up with demand.
Organizers set the face value, full stop. And no hidden fees, ever.

# How we'll work

Small slices, shipped to the pilots, and listen. The PO owns the product
decisions. The finance manager owns the money rules - if something in
this note disagrees with her, she's right. If it disagrees with what the
pilots actually need, they're right.
